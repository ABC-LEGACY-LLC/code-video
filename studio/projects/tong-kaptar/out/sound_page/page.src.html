2 → 3: what changed

You said the sound of shot 3 did not fit the picture. Measuring showed why: a random 14 Hz flutter in the wing grains, a narrow band (93 % of the energy below 1.2 kHz), and the beats of three out-of-focus birds filling the gaps between the white pigeon's beats. Now each beat is a “whoosh” that rises and falls with the wing's speed, with slowed feather crackle inside it and low air beneath it; the blurred birds around it are 20 dB lower and muffled; the echo of the slowed world is 1.6 s.

Your ear decides

The numbers find where a sound is wrong; they cannot prove that it is pleasant. I do not hear the sound, I only measure it. For each question compare with the New/Old buttons, and press M where you don't like it:

  1. Shot 3 (7.0–9.75 s): does each beat now sound as a “whooh” together with the wing in the picture?
  2. Take-off (0.9 s): the claps are papery, the feather hum 6 dB lower. Does it sound like a real pigeon taking off?
  3. Flock (4.2–7 s): a soft flutter, or does it sound like car noise?
  4. Coo (0.1 s, 5.6 s, 19.6 s): does it sound like a pigeon?
  5. Overall tone: version 3 has less in the low frequencies. Does it feel thin or dry?

Choices by convention, not from physics: the air noise on the turn (a camera move), shot 2's 4 dB rise, the blurred birds in shot 3 being 20 dB lower. The pigeons' cooing comes from inside the coop, because no pigeon in the picture coos.
